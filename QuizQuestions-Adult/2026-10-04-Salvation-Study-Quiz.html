  1. How does Scripture describe the salvation that comes through Jesus Christ? Consider the different tenses used for salvation.
    • As forgiveness already received, with no further saving work to come
    • As already received, being worked out, and still to come
      • These are phases of the same salvation. Ephesians 2:5–8; Romans 13:11; Philippians 2:12–13
    • As deliverance still to come, with no salvation already received
    • As moral growth now, with forgiveness reserved for the last day
  2. What did Jesus say to the woman who anointed His feet? Recall Jesus’ final words to her.
    • Her acts of love had settled the debt of her sins
    • Her sins were forgiven and her faith had saved her
      • Jesus declared her sins forgiven and told her that her faith had saved her. Luke 7:36–50
    • Her offering had secured the forgiveness she sought
    • Her devotion had made a declaration of forgiveness unnecessary
  3. How did Zacchaeus demonstrate repentance? Think about what he said concerning his possessions and past conduct.
    • He received Jesus without addressing his former dealings
    • He gave to the poor and promised restitution for false accusations
      • His generosity and restitution demonstrated his repentance. Luke 19:1–10
    • He appealed to his ancestry as evidence of a restored relationship
    • He offered future generosity in place of restoring what he had taken
  4. According to Ephesians 2, on what basis have we been saved? Look for the words Paul contrasts with works.
    • By grace through faith, as God’s gift
      • Salvation is God’s gift by grace through faith, not a basis for boasting in works. Ephesians 2:5–9
    • By grace in recognition of our righteous works
    • By faith together with the merit of keeping the law
    • By works that establish our worthiness to receive grace
  5. How does being made alive with Christ relate to forgiveness of sins? The passage describes people as dead in something specific.
    • Forgiveness follows only after we have removed our own trespasses
    • God makes us alive with Christ, forgiving our trespasses
      • The passages connect death in trespasses with condemnation and being made alive with forgiveness and reconciliation. Ephesians 2:5; 2 Corinthians 5:18–19
    • Being made alive restores opportunity, while forgiveness must still be earned
    • God overlooks trespasses without reconciling us through Christ
  6. According to Matthew 24:13, who will be saved? Consider Jesus’ words about faithfulness under pressure.
    • The one who endures to the end
      • Jesus said that the one who endures to the end will be saved. Matthew 10:22; Matthew 24:13
    • Belonging to the community that first received the gospel
    • Recognizing the signs that announce the end
    • Having begun faithfully before opposition arose
  7. According to Romans 5:9, what will those justified by Christ’s blood be saved from through Him? The verse distinguishes justification already received from a future deliverance.
    • The discipline described in 1 Corinthians 11
    • The wrath of God
      • Paul speaks of future salvation from wrath through Christ. Romans 5:9
    • The testing of ministry work by fire
    • The persecution described in Matthew 24
  8. Why are believers told to work out their salvation with fear and trembling? Consider both the believer’s responsibility and God’s activity.
    • Because our obedience must first persuade God to work in us
    • Because God works in us to will and to do His good pleasure
      • Believers are to yield to what God is working in them and obey His will. Philippians 2:12–13
    • Because salvation rests on our effort apart from God’s work
    • Because reverent obedience supplies what God’s work leaves unfinished
  9. What enables Christ to save completely those who come to God through Him? Think about Christ’s continuing ministry.
    • He always lives to make intercession for them
      • Christ is able to save to the uttermost because He always lives to intercede. Hebrews 7:25
    • He repeatedly offers Himself as a sacrifice for each new sin
    • He transfers His priesthood to successors who continue His work
    • He intercedes only until their first approach to God
  10. According to Revelation 12, how did the brethren overcome the accuser? Select all that apply. Select all answers that apply. More than one answer is correct.
    • By the blood of the Lamb
      • The passage names the Lamb’s blood, their testimony and their willingness to remain faithful even unto death. Revelation 12:10–11
    • By the word of their testimony
      • The passage names the Lamb’s blood, their testimony and their willingness to remain faithful even unto death. Revelation 12:10–11
    • By not loving their lives to the death
      • The passage names the Lamb’s blood, their testimony and their willingness to remain faithful even unto death. Revelation 12:10–11
    • By the accuser withdrawing his charges against them
  11. What matters most in remaining faithful to God through testing? Recall the discussion of Job and the test of commitment.
    • Relying on past obedience while setting aside present correction
    • Maintaining loyalty and love toward God
      • The emphasis is on the direction of one’s life and loyalty to God, not a simple count of daily deeds. Revelation 12:11
    • Counting religious achievements as proof that perseverance is secure
    • Treating freedom from opposition as evidence of God’s approval
  12. How should a believer respond after sinning, even deliberately? Remember the distinction between discipline and immediate rejection.
    • Wait until discipline has paid for the sin before returning
    • Offset the sin with good works before seeking forgiveness
    • Return to God in repentance
      • Believers should repent and return, while taking sin and God’s discipline seriously. 1 Corinthians 11:31–32
    • Rely on former faith without addressing the sin
  13. What warning does Hebrews give about persisting willfully in sin after receiving knowledge of the truth? Consider the warning that follows the call to hold fast and encourage one another.
    • There is no longer a sacrifice for sins, but an expectation of judgment
      • Hebrews warns of judgment for those who persist in rejecting the truth and treating the covenant with contempt. Hebrews 10:26–31
    • There will be loss of ministry reward without exposure to judgment
    • The former sacrifices provide an alternative to the rejected covenant
    • The warning concerns loss of earthly standing rather than divine judgment
  14. In 1 Corinthians 3:10–15, what work is tested by fire? Identify the workers, the building and the foundation in the passage.
    • The quality of a minister’s building on the foundation of Christ
      • Paul discusses the labor of ministers such as the one who plants and the one who waters, and the quality of their building. 1 Corinthians 3:5–15
    • The value of the gifts received rather than how the minister builds
    • The size of a following rather than the quality of what is built
    • The works by which the builder first earned reconciliation
  15. What does Cornelius’s example teach about the need to proclaim the gospel? Recall why Cornelius was told to send for Peter.
    • His prayers made Peter’s message unnecessary for salvation
    • He needed to hear words by which he and his household would be saved
      • Although Cornelius was described as righteous, he was directed to send for Peter and hear the message. Acts 11:13–14
    • His generosity supplied the salvation Peter would later confirm
    • Peter’s visit recognized salvation already secured by his good works
