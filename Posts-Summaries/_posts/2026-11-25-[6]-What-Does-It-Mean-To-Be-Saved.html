[6] What Does It Mean To Be Saved?

  1. Salvation: Past, Present and Future
    1. Scripture speaks of believers as having been saved, as being saved and as awaiting salvation. The ongoing and future descriptions can be grouped together because they point toward a salvation not yet complete [Ephesians 2:8; 1 Corinthians 1:18; Romans 13:11].
    2. Future salvation is secure as we continue in faith and obedience to God; it is not guaranteed regardless of how we live. Obedience means more than continuing to believe: it includes keeping God’s commands [Colossians 1:22–23; Hebrews 5:9].
    3. Salvation can be lost, but it is secure as we follow Christ and do God’s will to the best of our ability. Do not allow anyone to draw you into submission to the law of Moses [Galatians 5:2–4].
  2. Keeping God’s Commands Without Being Under the Law
    1. Circumcision and uncircumcision are nothing; keeping God’s commandments matters. Keeping His commands does not mean submitting to the law of Moses as a covenant [1 Corinthians 7:19].
    2. Obey every command of God that applies to you, regardless of the messenger through whom it came. Jesus submitted to John’s baptism to fulfill righteousness, even though John recognized Jesus’ greater standing [Matthew 3:13–15].
    3. Commands found in the law include loving God, loving your neighbor and honoring your father and mother. Paul applies the command to honor parents and its promise to believers [Deuteronomy 6:5; Leviticus 19:18; Ephesians 6:1–3].
    4. God’s revealed will does not cease to matter merely because the covenant changes. Peter applies the command to be holy, because God is holy, to believers [Leviticus 11:44–45; 1 Peter 1:15–16].
    5. The new covenant promises God’s law written on the heart. The point is not that God has changed His commands, but that His law is internalized under a different covenant [Jeremiah 31:31–33].
    6. Do not build a doctrine on a private application of a scriptural prophecy. Reference check for admin: review the application of 2 Peter 1:20 to this point.
  3. Reading the Law of Moses
    1. Read the law with an attitude of wanting to understand God’s will. Distinguish what applies literally from what applies figuratively or spiritually.
    2. Animal sacrifices are not offered literally by believers in the same repeated way. Christ’s sacrifice was offered once, unlike the sacrifices repeated under the law [Hebrews 10:1–14].
    3. Israel’s land was a particular inheritance, but the requirement of righteousness also applies to the inheritance associated with God’s kingdom. Reference check for admin: review the application to specific land allotted to individual believers.
  4. The Law as a Covenant
    1. The law of Moses is a covenant between God and Israel, beginning with the generation brought out of Egypt. Following a command found in that law does not by itself make someone subject to the covenant [Deuteronomy 5:2–3].
    2. Under the law, holiness involved not only righteousness but also particular covenant requirements. Circumcision, the Sabbaths and dietary distinctions were prominent requirements for Israel [Leviticus 12:3; Exodus 31:13; Leviticus 11:46–47].
  5. Circumcision and the Sabbaths
    1. Circumcision was the sign of God’s covenant with Abraham and was required of males in his household, including those born there or bought with money. It was also required for an Israelite male child under the law [Genesis 17:10–13; Leviticus 12:3].
    2. Circumcision alone does not place someone under the law of Moses. Ishmael was circumcised, and Paul circumcised Timothy because of the Jews in the places they would visit; Titus, a Greek, was not compelled to be circumcised [Genesis 17:23–26; Acts 16:1–3; Galatians 2:3].
    3. The purpose of circumcision matters: Timothy was not circumcised in order to submit to the law of Moses [Acts 16:3; Galatians 5:2–4].
    4. The Sabbaths were given as a sign between God and Israel throughout their generations. Those subject to that covenant were required to keep them [Exodus 31:13–17].
    5. A foreigner who wished to keep Passover with Israel had to have all his males circumcised; then he could partake as one born in the land [Exodus 12:48–49].
  6. The Seriousness of Submitting to the Law
    1. The law calls for continuing in what it commands, and Paul says that a man who accepts circumcision in the context of seeking justification by the law is obligated to keep the whole law [Deuteronomy 27:26; Galatians 5:2–4].
    2. Submitting to circumcision and observing a Sabbath as entry into the law is presented as binding a person to that covenant. For a Christian, submitting to the law in this way is a serious danger to life in Christ. Reference check for admin: review the proposed combination of circumcision and Sabbath observance as the criterion for entering the covenant [Galatians 5:2–4].
