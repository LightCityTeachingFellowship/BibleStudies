[3] What Does It Mean to Be Saved?

  1. Salvation and a living hope
    1. We are already saved, yet we also await salvation from God’s wrath. Present salvation and future deliverance should not be confused [Romans 5:9–10].
    2. In His mercy, God has given us new birth into a living hope through Jesus Christ’s resurrection. Our inheritance is imperishable, undefiled and kept in heaven; through faith we are shielded by God’s power for the salvation to be revealed in the last time [1 Peter 1:3–5].
    3. We rejoice in this hope even though, for a little while, we may suffer grief through various trials [1 Peter 1:6].
  2. Faith proved through trials
    1. Trials test the genuineness of faith, which is more precious than gold. Faith is proved through the tests we face now; its proven genuineness results in praise, glory and honor when Jesus Christ is revealed [1 Peter 1:6–7].
    2. Job’s perseverance is already recognized, and those listed in Hebrews were commended for their faith even though they had not yet received what was promised [James 5:10–11; Hebrews 11:32–40].
    3. The testing of faith produces perseverance. When perseverance completes its work, it contributes to maturity and completeness [James 1:2–4].
    4. Paul kept the faith and looked forward to the crown of righteousness, which the Lord will award to him and to all who have loved His appearing [2 Timothy 4:7–8].
  3. Justification, hope and perseverance
    1. Having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through Jesus Christ. Through Him we stand in grace and rejoice in hope of God’s glory [Romans 5:1–2].
    2. We also glory in tribulations, knowing that tribulation produces perseverance, perseverance produces proven character, and proven character produces hope. Hope does not disappoint, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit [Romans 5:3–5].
    3. Testing both proves faith and can improve us. We should endure rather than rebel against God’s correction [James 1:2–4; Hebrews 12:5–11].
  4. Reconciled now, saved from wrath in the future
    1. While we were still sinners, Christ died for us. We have now been justified by His blood and reconciled to God through His Son’s death; having been reconciled, we shall be saved from wrath through Him and saved by His life [Romans 5:8–10].
    2. Jesus’ death established the new covenant, the relationship in which we are joined to God [Hebrews 9:15–17].
    3. If God gave His Son to reconcile us while we were enemies, we can be confident that He will complete what He began. This assurance rests on God’s love, but it does not remove our responsibility to continue in faith [Romans 5:9–10; Colossians 1:21–23].
  5. The Spirit and God’s purpose
    1. The Holy Spirit is evidence of God’s love. The Spirit helps us in our weakness and intercedes for the saints according to God’s will [Romans 5:5; Romans 8:26–27].
    2. The Spirit’s ultimate value is not merely present miracles or breakthroughs: He helps bring us to the goal of salvation. The Spirit guides into truth and will give life to our mortal bodies [John 16:13; Romans 8:11].
    3. All things work together for good for those who love God and are called according to His purpose. In context, the good in view is not a promise of immediate wealth or that every present problem will turn out as we wish; it concerns being conformed to Christ and glorified [Romans 8:28–30].
    4. God’s purpose is that His Son be the firstborn among many brothers and sisters. Creation waits for the children of God to be revealed and to share in their glorious freedom [Romans 8:19–21, 29].
  6. More than conquerors through Christ
    1. God did not spare His own Son but gave Him up for us. God justifies, and the risen Christ intercedes for us; this encourages us in the challenges we face [Romans 8:31–34].
    2. Tribulation, persecution and other hardships cannot separate us from Christ’s love. In all these things we are more than conquerors through Him who loved us [Romans 8:35–39].
    3. The central battle is over reaching the promised glory. Standing firm in faith through hardship is itself a victory [1 Peter 1:6–7; Romans 8:37].
    4. The brethren overcome the accuser by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony, not loving their lives even to death. Mere suffering is not enough: we must truly believe and remain faithful to the gospel [Revelation 12:10–11].
    5. We must not become so accustomed to error that our discernment is dulled. Our response also teaches those who look to us, including the next generation.
  7. Correction and the goal of faith
    1. Faith is tested through hardship, including pressure to do wrong. A mistake does not mean immediate rejection: God disciplines and corrects His children, but we must not persist in rebellion [Hebrews 12:5–11].
    2. Jesus is the vine, and the Father is the gardener who removes branches that do not bear fruit. We must remain in Christ and bear fruit [John 15:1–6].
    3. Though we have not seen Jesus, we love and believe in Him, rejoicing as we receive the goal of faith: the salvation of our souls [1 Peter 1:8–9].
    4. This does not mean salvation is gradually imparted into the soul in increasing amounts. We already have salvation, and we await the future salvation of our souls when Christ comes [1 Peter 1:5, 9; Matthew 16:25–27].
