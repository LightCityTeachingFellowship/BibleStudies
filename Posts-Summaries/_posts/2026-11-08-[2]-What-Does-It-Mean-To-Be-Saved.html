[2] What Does It Mean To Be Saved?

  1. Present and future salvation
    1. The first phase of salvation is received through faith in Jesus. Believing with the heart brings righteousness; it does not require an oral confession or a work of righteousness [Romans 10:10; Ephesians 2:8–9].
    2. Romans 10 distinguishes believing with the heart unto righteousness from confessing with the mouth unto salvation. The latter concerns future salvation and is not merely a one-time declaration: it is a lifetime of acknowledging Jesus as Lord and calling upon Him [Romans 10:10–13].
    3. At Cornelius’ house, Peter proclaimed forgiveness through faith in Jesus, and the Holy Spirit came upon those listening before they were baptized in water [Acts 10:43–48].
    4. Scripture speaks of salvation as already received: Jesus told the woman who wiped His feet that her sins were forgiven and that her faith had saved her. He also declared that salvation had come to Zacchaeus’ house after Zacchaeus expressed repentance through generosity and restitution [Luke 7:48–50; 19:8–9].
  2. Made alive from trespasses and sins
    1. We were dead in trespasses and sins, but God, rich in mercy and love, made us alive together with Christ. In this context, being made alive includes being delivered from our trespasses through forgiveness; it is salvation by grace [Ephesians 2:1–5, 8].
    2. Sin was in the world before the law of Moses, but sin was not counted where there was no law. Death nevertheless reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sin was not like Adam’s transgression [Romans 5:12–14].
    3. The law entered so that the trespass might increase: a command makes sin an offense against God. For example, failing to love a neighbor is an offense against God because He commands us to love our neighbor [Romans 5:20; Leviticus 19:18].
    4. Paul calls the covenant engraved on stones a ministry of death and condemnation, while the ministry of the Spirit and righteousness has greater glory [2 Corinthians 3:7–9].
  3. Faith, repentance and works
    1. Both the beginning and the completion of salvation require faith. We begin by believing in Jesus and must continue in faith rather than turning to the law of Moses as the basis for our salvation [Romans 1:16–17].
    2. At the beginning, repentance is assumed when a person believes; it need not first be demonstrated by works. At Cornelius’ house, the Spirit came while Peter was still speaking, before baptism [Acts 10:43–48].
    3. For future salvation, repentance must be demonstrated. Paul preached that people should repent, turn to God and prove their repentance by their deeds [Acts 26:19–20].
    4. Works are checked to show whether faith and loyalty to Jesus are genuine, not to measure whether a person has accumulated enough works. Christ will reward each person according to their deeds [Revelation 22:12].
    5. Baptism is a pledge of a good conscience toward God: a commitment to do what is right before Him. It saves through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, who has gone into heaven and is at God’s right hand [1 Peter 3:21–22].
  4. Jesus’ death, resurrection and intercession
    1. The first phase of salvation depends on Jesus’ blood and death. His resurrection is also necessary: if Christ has not been raised, faith is futile and we remain in our sins [Ephesians 1:7; 1 Corinthians 15:16–17].
    2. For future salvation, we need the living Christ’s intercession. He has entered heaven and is able to save those who come to God through Him because He always lives to intercede for them [Hebrews 7:25; 9:24].
  5. God’s commandments and the law of Moses
    1. The first salvation requires faith rather than prior obedience to commandments. For the future phase, obedience matters: circumcision and uncircumcision are nothing, but keeping God’s commandments matters [1 Corinthians 7:19].
    2. Keeping God’s commandments does not mean placing ourselves under the law of Moses as a covenant. The righteous requirements of the law can be kept by one who is uncircumcised; in Christ, what matters is faith working through love [Romans 2:25–27; Galatians 5:6].
    3. God’s will is revealed in the law. We should study it to learn His will, distinguish sin from what is not sin, and put His will into practice. Paul says the law made sin known and that the commandment is holy, just and good [Romans 7:7, 12].
    4. Love your neighbor as yourself is a command from the law of Moses. The commandments are summed up in this saying, so walking in love expresses the law’s righteous requirements [Leviticus 19:18; Romans 13:9; Galatians 5:14].
    5. Justification by faith does not nullify the law; it upholds it. Paul appeals to Abraham to show that righteousness by faith is consistent with the Scriptures, including the books of Moses [Romans 3:31–4:3].
  6. Applying God’s will
    1. Paul teaches order and submission in the churches, including instructions for women to be silent and to learn in submission. He presents his instructions as the Lord’s command and appeals to the law and the creation of Adam and Eve [1 Corinthians 14:34–37; 1 Timothy 2:11–14].
    2. Reference check for admin: 1 Corinthians 14:34 refers to what ‘the law says,’ but the exact law passage for that instruction was not specified.
    3. Christ’s teaching on marriage and divorce appeals to God’s design in the beginning: what God has joined together should not be separated [Matthew 19:4–6; 1 Corinthians 7:10–11].
    4. Children are to honor their father and mother. Paul applies the command and its promise to believers, speaking of living long on the earth; Jesus also rebuked the practice of withholding support from parents under the guise of devotion to God [Ephesians 6:1–3; Mark 7:9–13].
    5. Even when parents cause difficulty, honor them and commit the matter to God rather than praying for their destruction.
  7. Faith that obeys
    1. The future phase of salvation is not simply settled regardless of how we live. Our works must show that we truly believe Jesus is Lord and submit to Him.
    2. The obedience that comes from faith means finding out the Lord’s will and doing it, not merely repeating that we believe. Learn what pleases Him from the Scriptures and put it into practice [Romans 1:5; Ephesians 5:10].
