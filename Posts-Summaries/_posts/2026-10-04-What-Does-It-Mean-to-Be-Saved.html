What Does It Mean to Be Saved?

  1. Salvation in Scripture
    1. The question of whether salvation can be lost must begin with what Scripture means by salvation. Saying a sinner’s prayer should not be treated as permission to continue in sin or as proof that a believer’s actions cannot be sinful. [Romans 6:1–2; Hebrews 10:26–29].
    2. Scripture speaks of salvation as already accomplished, as being worked out, and as still to come. These are phases of the same salvation, not unrelated salvations. [Ephesians 2:5–8; Philippians 2:12–13; Romans 13:11].
  2. The Salvation Already Received
    1. Jesus told the woman who anointed His feet that her sins were forgiven and that her faith had saved her. Her great love reflected her recognition of the forgiveness she had received. [Luke 7:36–50].
    2. Salvation came to Zacchaeus’s house as he demonstrated repentance, giving to the poor and restoring what he had taken by false accusation. Repentance is required for forgiveness. [Luke 19:1–10; Acts 3:19].
    3. God made us alive with Christ when we were dead in trespasses. We have been saved by grace through faith, not by our works; God saved us according to His purpose and mercy. [Ephesians 2:1–9; 2 Timothy 1:9; Titus 3:5].
    4. To be dead in trespasses is to be under condemnation. God reconciles us through Christ by not counting our trespasses against us. This forgiveness is received in Christ through faith, not automatically by everyone. [Colossians 2:13; 2 Corinthians 5:18–21; Romans 5:1].
  3. The Salvation Still to Come
    1. Jesus said that those who endure to the end will be saved. Paul likewise spoke of those already justified by Christ’s blood being saved from wrath and of salvation drawing nearer than when they first believed. [Matthew 24:13; Romans 5:9; Romans 13:11].
    2. Christ will appear again to bring salvation to those waiting for Him. We are to work out our salvation with fear and trembling, because God works in us to will and to do His good pleasure. [Hebrews 9:28; Philippians 2:12–13].
    3. Christ is able to save completely those who come to God through Him, since He always lives to intercede for them. Eternal salvation is for those who obey Him. [Hebrews 7:25; Hebrews 5:9].
  4. Intercession, Endurance and Loyalty
    1. The devil remains an accuser, but Christ died, rose and intercedes for His people. In Revelation, the brethren overcome by the blood of the Lamb, by the word of their testimony and by not loving their lives to the death. [Romans 8:34; Revelation 12:10–11].
    2. Holding to the gospel includes obeying it. The test is not a daily count of good deeds against bad deeds, but the direction of our lives and our loyalty and love toward God. [1 Corinthians 15:1–2; Revelation 12:11].
    3. The salvation already received brings reconciliation; the salvation to come brings deliverance from wrath. If we fail to obtain the latter, the purpose for which we were reconciled is lost. It is possible, though not easy, to lose salvation by refusing to remain loyal to God. [Romans 5:9–10; Hebrews 3:12–14].
  5. Sin, Repentance and Discipline
    1. Believers can still sin, sometimes even deliberately. This is not permission to sin: God calls us to repent and may discipline us to bring us back rather than condemn us with the world. [1 John 1:8–9; 1 Corinthians 11:31–32].
    2. David knowingly sinned, yet God forgave him and also disciplined him. A believer should return to God in repentance rather than assume that one sin means immediate rejection. [2 Samuel 12:9–14; 1 John 1:9].
    3. Sickness or hardship can be connected to sin, but not every sickness or hardship is caused by sin. If a person persistently refuses correction and continues in sin, Hebrews warns of judgment. [James 5:14–16; John 9:1–3; Hebrews 10:26–31].
    4. The requirement is not a life with no sin at all, but perfect loyalty to God. Yield to His word and to His work in you. [Philippians 2:12–13; Hebrews 3:12–14].
  6. Works, Ministry and the Gospel
    1. In 1 Corinthians 3, the work tested by fire concerns how ministers build on the foundation of Christ and the reward for their labor. It is not the same discussion as obedience and righteousness in the believer’s walk. [1 Corinthians 3:5–15].
    2. Reconciliation begins through faith in Christ, not through righteous works. Cornelius was a righteous man, yet he was directed to hear words by which he and his household would be saved. Therefore, preach the gospel. [Acts 10:1–4; Acts 11:13–14].
